### 1. The Physical Model: Vibrating String (Page 63)
The course derives the wave equation using a **vibrating string** fixed at both ends ($x=0$ and $x=l$).
*   **$u(x,t)$**: The displacement (amplitude) of the string at position $x$ and time $t$.
*   **$v$**: The speed with which a disturbance travels along the string (determined by tension and linear density).
*   **The 1D Classical Wave Equation:**
    $$ \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial x^2} = \frac{1}{v^2} \frac{\partial^2 u}{\partial t^2} \quad \text{(Eq. 2.1 / 3.1)} $$
*   **Classification**: It is a **linear partial differential equation** (PDE) because $u$ and its derivatives appear only to the first power with no cross-terms.

### 2. Boundary Conditions (Page 63)
Because the string ends are fixed, the solution *must* satisfy:
$$ u(0,t) = 0 \quad \text{and} \quad u(l,t) = 0 \quad \text{for all } t $$
These constraints lead to **quantization** (discrete allowed frequencies/modes), a concept that carries directly into quantum mechanics.

### 3. Solution Method: Separation of Variables (Pages 66, 97)
The text emphasizes this method as the standard approach used later for the Schrödinger equation.

**Step 1: Assume a Product Solution**
$$ u(x,t) = \psi(x) \cos(\omega t) \quad \text{(Eq. 3.2)} $$
*   $\psi(x)$: **Spatial amplitude** (function of position only).
*   $\cos(\omega t)$: Harmonic time dependence (angular frequency $\omega$).

**Step 2: Substitute into PDE**
Substituting Eq. 3.2 into the wave equation (Eq. 3.1) yields an **Ordinary Differential Equation (ODE) for the spatial part** (Eq. 3.3):
$$ \frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + \frac{\omega^2}{v^2}\psi(x) = 0 $$
Letting $k = \omega/v$ (wave number), this becomes the **Helmholtz equation**:
$$ \frac{d^2\psi}{dx^2} + k^2\psi = 0 $$

**Step 3: General Solution of the Spatial ODE (Page 66)**
Since this is a linear, 2nd-order ODE with constant coefficients, the general solution is a linear combination of two independent solutions:
$$ \psi(x) = c_1 e^{ikx} + c_2 e^{-ikx} $$
Or, equivalently (using Euler's formula), a superposition of sine and cosine waves:
$$ \psi(x) = A \sin(kx) + B \cos(kx) $$
*   **Constants $c_1, c_2$ (or $A, B$)**: Arise from the two integrations required to solve a 2nd-order ODE.
*   **Linearity**: The sum of solutions is a solution (Superposition Principle).

**Step 4: Apply Boundary Conditions $\rightarrow$ Quantization**
Applying $u(0,t)=0 \Rightarrow \psi(0)=0$ forces $B=0$.
Applying $u(l,t)=0 \Rightarrow \psi(l)=0$ forces $k l = n\pi$ ($n=1,2,3...$).
This yields the **Normal Modes**:
$$ \psi_n(x) = A \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{l}\right) $$
$$ u_n(x,t) = A \sin\left(\frac{n\pi x}{l}\right) \cos(\omega_n t) $$
