**Tutor:** # Wave Motion: A Fundamental Physics Concept

Wave motion is one of the most fundamental and ubiquitous phenomena in physics. It describes how **energy and information propagate through space without the net transport of matter**.

---

## 🌊 Core Definition
A **wave** is a disturbance that travels through a medium (or vacuum, for electromagnetic waves), transferring energy from one point to another while the particles of the medium oscillate about their equilibrium positions.

**Key Insight:** The *wave* moves forward; the *medium* (mostly) stays in place.

---

## 📐 Essential Wave Parameters

| Parameter | Symbol | Unit | Description |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Amplitude** | $A$ | meters (m) | Maximum displacement from equilibrium. Related to **energy/intensity**. |
| **Wavelength** | $\lambda$ | meters (m) | Distance between two consecutive points in phase (e.g., crest-to-crest). |
| **Frequency** | $f$ | Hertz (Hz = s⁻¹) | Number of oscillations per second. Determined by the **source**. |
| **Period** | $T$ | seconds (s) | Time for one complete oscillation ($T = 1/f$). |
| **Wave Speed** | $v$ | m/s | Speed at which the wave profile travels. Determined by the **medium**. |

### The Fundamental Wave Relationship
$$v = f \lambda = \frac{\lambda}{T}$$
*   **$v$ depends on medium properties** (tension, density, elasticity, refractive index).
*   **$f$ depends on the source**.
*   **$\lambda$ adjusts** to satisfy the equation when a wave enters a new medium.

---

## 🔄 Two Fundamental Classifications

### 1. By Particle Motion vs. Propagation Direction
| Type | Particle Motion | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Transverse Waves** | Perpendicular ($\perp$) to propagation | Light (EM waves), waves on a string, S-waves (seismic) |
| **Longitudinal Waves** | Parallel ($\parallel$) to propagation | Sound waves in air/fluids, P-waves (seismic), compression waves in a spring |

> **Note:** Surface water waves are a **combination** (orbital motion).

### 2. By Medium Requirement
| Type | Requires Medium? | Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mechanical Waves** | **Yes** | Elastic restoring forces + inertia (e.g., sound, water waves, seismic). |
| **Electromagnetic Waves** | **No** (travel in vacuum) | Oscillating electric & magnetic fields sustaining each other (Maxwell's Equations). |

## 📝 Mathematical Description (1D Traveling Wave)

A sinusoidal wave traveling in the $+x$ direction:
$$y(x, t) = A \sin(kx - \omega t + \phi)$$

*   **Wave Number:** $k = \frac{2\pi}{\lambda}$ (spatial frequency)
*   **Angular Frequency:** $\omega = 2\pi f = \frac{2\pi}{T}$ (temporal frequency)
*   **Phase Constant:** $\phi$ (initial condition)
*   **Phase Velocity:** $v_p = \frac{\omega}{k}$

**Traveling in $-x$ direction:** $y(x, t) = A \sin(kx + \omega t + \phi)$

---

## ⚡ Energy & Power in Waves

*   **Energy Density** (energy per unit volume/length) $\propto A^2 \omega^2$.
*   **Intensity** (Power per unit area, $I$) for a spherical wave: $I = \frac{P}{4\pi r^2} \propto \frac{1}{r^2}$ (Inverse Square Law).
*   **Wave Impedance ($Z$):** Ratio of driving force to velocity (or pressure to particle velocity). Determines **reflection/transmission** at boundaries.

---

## 🎭 Key Wave Phenomena

| Phenomenon | Description | Condition/Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reflection** | Wave bounces off boundary | Fixed end: $\pi$ phase flip. Free end: no flip. |
| **Refraction** | Change in direction/speed at boundary | Snell's Law: $n_1 \sin\theta_1 = n_2 \sin\theta_2$ |
| **Diffraction** | Bending around obstacles/apertures | Significant when $\lambda \approx \text{aperture size}$ |
| **Interference** | Superposition of waves | Constructive ($\Delta \phi = 2m\pi$), Destructive ($\Delta \phi = (2m+1)\pi$) |
| **Standing Waves** | Interference of two identical waves traveling oppositely | Nodes (zero amplitude) & Antinodes (max amplitude). Resonance condition: $L = n\frac{\lambda}{2}$ |
| **Doppler Effect** | Frequency shift due to relative motion | $f' = f \frac{v \pm v_o}{v \mp v_s}$ |
| **Dispersion** | Wave speed depends on frequency ($v(\omega)$) | Pulse broadening; Phase velocity $\neq$ Group velocity ($v_g = d\omega/dk$) |

## 🧪 Speed of Mechanical Waves (Examples)

1.  **String under tension $T$, linear density $\mu$:**
    $$v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}}$$
2.  **Sound in a fluid (bulk modulus $B$, density $\rho$):**
    $$v = \sqrt{\frac{B}{\rho}}$$
3.  **Sound in a solid (Young's modulus $Y$, density $\rho$):**
    $$v = \sqrt{\frac{Y}{\rho}}$$
4.  **Sound in ideal gas (adiabatic index $\gamma$, molar mass $M$, temp $T$):**
    $$v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma RT}{M}}$$

## 💡 Why Wave Motion Matters
*   **Communication:** Radio, fiber optics, 5G, WiFi (EM waves).
*   **Medical Imaging:** Ultrasound (sound waves), MRI (radio waves), X-rays.
*   **Energy:** Seismic exploration, ocean wave energy harvesting.
*   **Quantum Mechanics:** Wave-particle duality ($\psi(x,t)$), de Broglie wavelength $\lambda = h/p$.
*   **Fundamental Forces:** Gravitational waves (ripples in spacetime).

---

## 🤔 Common Student Misconceptions
1.  **"Waves carry matter."** → They carry *energy/momentum*; matter oscillates locally.
2.  **"Wave speed depends on frequency."** → Only in **dispersive** media (e.g., light in glass, water waves). In non-dispersive media (sound in air, light in vacuum), $v$ is constant.
3.  **"Amplitude affects wave speed."** → Only for **non-linear** waves (shock waves, solitons). For linear (small amplitude) waves, $v$ is independent of $A$.
